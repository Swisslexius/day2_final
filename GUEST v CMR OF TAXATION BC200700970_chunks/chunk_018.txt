question be identified. The ordinary meaning of the language is that it is sufficient that the person who made the 
representation, whoever he or she is, had or might reasonably be supposed to have had, personal knowledge of the 
asserted fact. The policy behind the provision is clear enough. Routine business records, made before any legal 
proceeding arises or is contemplated (cf the exception in s 69(3)), have an inherent likelihood of reliability which 
outweighs the common law’s aversion to hearsay evidence where the maker of a statement cannot be tested by 
cross-examination.
