[81]  Their Honours (at [10]) rejected the Commissioner’s argument that the taxpayer only became obliged to pay 
interest in the years in question because she chose not to repay the principal sum and that the “occasion” for the 
interest repayment was each periodic decision to keep the loan on foot. They did so for two reasons. First, the loan 
was for a fixed term and was not dependent on periodic decisions on the taxpayer’s part to keep it alive. Secondly, 
she did not have the financial capacity to do so. Their Honours noted (at [11]) that “the borrowed funds and the 
interest on them were always referrable to the former business”
[82]  Their Honours (at [14]) observed that Riverside Rd:
… does not establish that a taxpayer who has a legal right to repay a business loan after the cessation of business can 
never thereafter deduct interest payable on the loan.
